SCHEMA

[Continuous Wave (CW) Pump Laser]
        ↓ (Single-frequency light)
[Optical Coupler]  <-- (Connects laser to microresonator)
        ↓
[Microresonator (e.g., Ring Resonator on a chip)]
        ↓ (Inside the microresonator: Nonlinear optical effects)
        ↓ (CW light transforms into a stable Soliton pulse)
        ↓ (Soliton generates a broad Frequency Comb)
[Optical Coupler]  <-- (Extracts light from microresonator)
        ↓
[Output: Soliton Frequency Comb]
        (Many equally-spaced, phase-coherent optical frequencies)


  Explanation:


   1. Continuous Wave (CW) Pump Laser: This is the input. It's a standard, single-frequency laser
      that provides a steady, unmodulated beam of light.
   2. Optical Coupler: A passive component (like a waveguide or a prism) that efficiently transfers
      light from the pump laser into the microresonator.
   3. Microresonator: This is the heart of the system. It's a tiny, high-quality optical cavity
      (often a ring or disk shape on a silicon chip) where light circulates many times.
   4. Nonlinear Effects & Soliton Generation: When the CW laser light circulates within the
      microresonator, its intensity builds up. Due to the material's optical nonlinearity, the
      light self-organizes into a stable, localized pulse called a dissipative Kerr soliton.
   5. Frequency Comb Generation: This circulating soliton pulse, in turn, generates a broad
      spectrum of new, equally-spaced optical frequencies (the frequency comb). It's like a single
      laser input creating hundreds of new, perfectly synchronized lasers.
   6. Output: The frequency comb is then coupled out of the microresonator, ready for use in
      applications like optical interconnects.
